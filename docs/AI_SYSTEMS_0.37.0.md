# Frontier Lab 0.37.0 — AI Systems

AI Systems adds three operating designs, twelve research discoveries, four practical challenge formats, renewable customer commissions, and a connected model-development workspace. Build and content version are **0.37.0**. The game-state schema remains **5**.

The expansion gives different designs different jobs to do. A small service model can compete on its bill and footprint, a persistent-state model can retain a changing case, and an adaptive reasoner can pay for stronger checking. Existing conventional models, size families, training methods, customers, and campaign ambitions remain available.

These are authored simulation rules. Parameter counts, memory, serving work, retention, verification, and response-time multipliers are game measurements used to make decisions comparable. The game does not train or run actual neural networks, and its figures are not claims about real AI performance, hardware requirements, or safety.

## Three operating designs

Choose an operating design when creating a new custom foundation. It is separate from the existing size family, parameter count, data mix, attention, precision, and training objective.

| Operating design | Choices | Strength | Cost or limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Expert network | Four, eight, or sixteen experts; one, two, or four active experts; uniform, learned, or balanced routing; a specialty | Uses a smaller active portion of the network for each request and can favor a selected skill | All weights remain resident. Larger banks need more evidence and preparation; routing quality and the number of active experts affect reliability, generality, and work. Requires compatible sparse infrastructure and fast fabric. |
| Persistent-state model | Sixteen, sixty-four, or 256 state slots; fixed, compressed, or selective memory; contradiction repair | Retains useful information across the authored long-running workloads, with a smaller resident-weight footprint | State is charged for every simultaneous account. Compression can discard detail, and repair adds evidence and inference costs. Static skill breadth has tradeoffs. |
| Adaptive reasoning | Two, four, or eight maximum passes; single or branching search; self or independent checking; fixed or confidence stopping | Uses trained reasoning and additional work to improve verified completion | More work raises serving cost and response time. Independent checking has a memory and evidence cost. Early stopping reduces average effort while giving up some completion performance. |

Research unlocks these controls for future paid foundations. It does not retrofit existing models. Further training preserves the source model's operating protocol and its paid resource effects; changing the protocol requires another foundation. Expert routing does not stack a second discount on top of the old free-form sparsity control.

The operating-profile display shows standard serving work, resident weights, per-account memory, retention, verified completion, and response time. Serving work and account memory use the same measurements as ordinary product service. A model with weak trained reasoning cannot gain useful verified completion merely by selecting an expensive checker.

## Practical challenges and renewable customers

The three original combined-skill challenges remain intact. Four new paid systems challenges each contain **six fixed workloads** and award a title for passing **five**. A workload requires the appropriate trained skills and reliability as well as its operating limit.

| Challenge | What it measures | Related renewable commission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost | Useful support, document, and tool work within a serving-compute limit | Expert service desk |
| Memory | Useful local assistance within the complete model-and-session footprint | Local knowledge assistant |
| Retention | Earlier constraints and revised facts across a continuing case, with bounded memory | Case continuity service |
| Verified completion | Checked answers and tool outcomes within a response-time budget | Verified analysis desk |

Every requirement is shown before the player runs a test. Forecast comparisons use the same rules as the paid benchmark, but a forecast does not award a report or title. A challenge requires prior paid capability evaluation of that exact model, then consumes its own development compute and Research points. Renaming or repeating unchanged ability cannot reroll its cases. Challenge evidence does not replace a release test.

These routes check measured results rather than a prescribed model name. A conventional compact foundation can qualify for constrained work. A large persistent state can help retention while failing a small-memory workload. Always using the maximum reasoning budget can miss a response deadline even when it improves completion.

### Commission terms

Four customer offers appear in deterministic **12-week windows**. Their clients, workload scales, fees, and some requirements vary between windows. All offers remain visible while locked.

- Choose **one systems commission per window** and accept it during the window's first **eight weeks**.
- Select an owned model that meets the listed requirements, has hosted-use rights, and already holds the required paid passing challenge report.
- Deliver **two consecutive weeks** of complete client work within **six weeks** of acceptance.
- Reserve the client's serving compute and memory alongside ordinary customers. The reference footprint includes one model copy and **25 simultaneous sessions**.
- The client pays once after successful delivery. A shortfall resets the consecutive-week streak; a missed deadline earns no fee. A later window offers another opportunity.

Acceptance freezes the client, model, report, fee, requirements, and deadline. Later windows cannot reprice an active order. Existing one-time briefs and systems commissions share the one-active-commission rule. Unique order, work-proof, and payment records prevent accepting the same window again or collecting a fee twice. One-time delivery fees increase spendable cash without becoming recurring runway income.

Active client work appears in the home spotlight and Studio attention, with a direct route to its capacity requirements. Results and delivery history retain completed and expired orders.

## One development workspace

Open **Projects → Develop a model** and select a model purpose, systems challenge, or renewable customer target. The working objective follows the player through manual design, research, datasets, and delegated development.

Manual and delegated modes expose the same objective and preserve the selected design and data. Delegation connects preparation, training, and evaluation under the existing budget, reserve, and deadline limits, with a final review of the completed model. The player can still manage individual steps manually.

The forecast compares expected results with the selected workloads and customer requirements. Research suggestions follow the objective and include conventional alternatives such as attention and precision improvements for a memory or cost target. Locked choices explain their research requirements and remain discoverable. Routine scheduling uses the existing direct-action flow; this expansion does not add confirmation screens to each research or design choice.

The objective is remembered with the browser's model workspace, alongside drafts and blueprints. It is planning metadata, not a paid command or an earned result. Portable game snapshots retain the actual model, research, evaluation, and customer evidence.

## Save compatibility

Upgrading a valid published 0.36 campaign changes its content and build headers. It preserves paid model designs, active training plans, model outcomes, licenses, reports, company money, random streams, and existing market plans. New designs use optional version-one system specifications and frozen effects. Legacy designs continue using their original rules; absence of a system specification retains conventional behavior.

The first successful upgraded browser save archives the exact original 0.36 bytes before replacing the primary save. If the archive cannot be written, the previous primary remains intact. Existing compressed-save bounds and recovery behavior remain in force.

Validation rejects new system designs, A1–A12 research, systems challenges, and systems orders disguised under an older content header. It also rejects altered paid protocols, missing evaluation work, changed accepted terms, unpaid claimed delivery, and orphaned commission proofs. In particular, an unfinished or expired order cannot simply be removed while its signed or delivered-work evidence remains.
