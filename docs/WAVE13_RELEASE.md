# Wave 13 — Assured API contracts

## Release intent and implemented scope

Wave 13 connects the paid runtime audits from Wave 12 to a new enterprise product decision. An owned Developer API can accept a finite buyer's usefulness and restricted-compliance requirements, fund commercial onboarding, and earn an eight-week retainer by delivering a separate tenant workload and recurring support. The player must keep the accepted model configuration and checker applicable while preserving enough GPU capacity and commercial staff to honor the promise.

Content and build version are **0.14.0**; save schema remains **5**. New contracts and invoices are optional records created only by explicit adoption. Existing research, models, products, prices, randomness, and saved economic history continue unchanged when assurance contracts are unused.

The interface keeps the Universal Paperclips style: **Assured API contracts** is an expandable section within Developer API, with two buyer offers, ordinary action buttons, compact service metrics, accepted-evidence details, and invoice history. The same deterministic engine provides previews, prerequisites, charges, capacity admission, and receipts. There is no separate UI economy.

## Buyers and prerequisites

Signing requires completed **Enterprise operations (R12)** and the runtime-guardrail research prerequisites, an owned active Developer API, and its exact active, available paid runtime audit. Every model route must retain its commercial policy eligibility and inherited hosted-use rights. The accepted primary model, routing secondary, features, checker profile, audit identity, and tenant meter are captured together.

The two authored buyers consume the same finite business population as existing cohorts, trials, and ordinary enterprise commitments:

| Buyer | Useful allowed answers required | Restricted compliances allowed | Requests per week | Full weekly retainer | Commercial support per week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Continuity Systems | At least 42 of 50 | At most 2 of 50 | 4,000 | $1,200 | 0.25 FTE |
| Sentinel Services | At least 40 of 50 | 0 of 50 | 8,000 | $2,200 | 0.50 FTE |

These requirements apply to the actual finite audit outcomes. Buyers are not hard-coded to a particular checker profile. A different model or route can produce another qualifying combination. In the earned company used for this release, balanced checking produces **43 useful answers and 2 restricted compliances**, qualifying for Continuity; strict checking produces **41 useful answers and 0 restricted compliances**, qualifying for Sentinel. Each misses the other buyer's requirement, creating an operating tradeoff.

The audit remains the same paid set of 50 allowed and 50 restricted cases. Signing or requalifying does not reroll it, produce another model evaluation, or improve model weights. A zero observed compliance count is evidence about those reserved cases, not a general guarantee about future behavior. The contracted traffic is a separate benign tenant workload; the game does not create restricted production requests or a new policy-incident simulator in this wave.

## Paid onboarding and fixed terms

Every new term pays a **$750 integration expense** at command commit and requires **one commercial staff-week** of onboarding. Progress uses actual independently granted commercial effort, capped at one staff-week per week. No productive staff means no progress. Partial allocation accumulates over successive weeks; it cannot create a term or revenue before the required work is complete.

Completed onboarding becomes ready for the following boundary. At that boundary the contract becomes active and receives a fixed expiry **eight weeks after its start**. Those eight calendar weeks include weeks of suspended or incomplete service. The term does not restart because the checker is disabled, an API is paused, GPU capacity is insufficient, or a new qualification is accepted.

Each active week creates one invoice, including a zero-dollar invoice when a full service credit applies. After the eighth service week, the next boundary marks the contract expired, releases future resource obligations, and retains its original term, accepted evidence, usage receipts, and earned invoices. There is no automatic renewal.

An explicit cancellation ends future serving, support, and retainers at its command boundary. Cancelling during unfinished onboarding retains the paid integration expense and actual progress but starts no term and earns no invoice. Cancelling an active term preserves invoices already earned. A buyer remains counted during the ending week and returns to the outside market at the following boundary; a new term requires another ordinary signing command, fee, and onboarding effort.

The history is bounded at **four terms per buyer, eight contracts overall, four accepted qualifications per contract, and 64 invoices overall**. Those bounds retain the complete supported history instead of deleting earlier acceptance or billing evidence to make room for another term.

## Exact qualification and configuration changes

The first qualification records the signing command, acceptance week, installation ID, profile, complete source binding, evidence and audit fingerprints, finite audit totals, and separate tenant meter. Later acceptance appends a new chronological version. Prior versions and their invoices remain unchanged.

Service requires the current active checker to be the exact accepted installation with the same binding and audit. The following actions therefore have distinct consequences:

| Operating choice | Contract consequence |
|---|---|
| Disable the accepted checker | Inference stops, the retainer receives a full credit, and the original calendar continues |
| Reenable that same matching checker | Service can resume with its existing qualification |
| Pause the public API | Tenant inference stops while the calendar and recurring support obligation continue |
| Restore the API's active state | Service can resume if the accepted checker still matches |
| Change model, actual route, or conversation features | The old binding becomes inapplicable; a matching paid checker is required |
| Activate a different checker installation | The tenant requires explicit acceptance of that qualifying installation |
| Accept a current qualifying audit | Append a new exact qualification and meter; preserve the original expiry and historical invoices |
| Change public prefix-cache warmth or its operating toggle | The uncached tenant promise and accepted finite audit remain unchanged |

Explicit requalification is free because the runtime installation and audit were already paid. It still undergoes current buyer, product, policy, rights, and capacity checks. It cannot accept an unfinished audit, duplicate the current installation, exceed the retained qualification bound, or extend an expired term. Historical validation uses the version accepted in each invoice's service week, rather than substituting the newest version into old receipts.

## GPU capacity, residency, and staff

Each contract has its own serving request and resident workspace. The accepted meter combines ordinary model work for the captured features, the actual routing share and routing overhead when present, and the selected runtime-checker work. Both resident models are included for a routed product, together with its session workspace and checker memory. Routes require compatible architectures and a pool that can run both; sparse workloads retain their fabric requirement.

Assurance tenants do not inherit the public product's warm prefix cache or share an adapter workspace with another request. Their promises remain visible while onboarding or suspended. Admission considers existing public work, private deployments, accepted assurance promises, and queued private or assurance choices. An explicitly queued requalification replaces that contract's old meter under the same serving request ID; it is counted once at its new size before another contract can be admitted.

Serving reserves **10% headroom** through the existing allocator. Effective work is converted to the actual physical GPU work granted by the selected hardware, with the usual rounded reservation and separate memory checks. Partial execution consumes its actual work even when it is insufficient to complete another whole request. Completed requests alone contribute complete request and token counts.

Commercial support is an independent weekly request: 0.25 FTE for Continuity or 0.5 FTE for Sentinel. It competes with existing commercial work and is not supplied implicitly by compute capacity. A staffed contract with no available GPUs earns no retainer; a fully executed workload with no commercial support also earns no retainer. Support remains an obligation during suspension until cancellation or expiry.

Actual serving grants enter the ordinary company-hosted compute ledger. Owned GPUs incur their normal variable operating costs; cloud work consumes rented reservation rather than inventing a second per-request rent bill. The contract receives an **assured-api** activity in cost accounting, and physical tenant traffic contributes to recorded private serving demand. Cost reports reconcile these activities, public products, and shared expenses to the same company totals.

## Retainers, proportional credits, and usage boundaries

The authored service target is **98%**. Billing follows proportional delivery, without an additional SLA fine or a threshold that makes near-complete service free. The integer calculation is:

1. Divide executed effective work by accepted work per request and round down to completed requests.
2. Calculate request fulfillment and commercial-support fulfillment in basis points, each capped at 10,000.
3. Set qualification fulfillment to 10,000 only when the API is active and the exact accepted checker applies; otherwise use zero.
4. Use the lowest of those three fulfillment values as the paid service fraction.
5. Multiply the weekly retainer by that fraction, rounding down to whole cents. Credit the remainder of the promised retainer.

For example, half delivery under an otherwise valid and fully staffed Continuity contract earns **$600** and records **$600** of service credit. The same result follows from half support with all requests completed. A disabled or stale accepted checker gives a zero service fraction and full credit. GPU execution stops for such a suspended tenant; the separate support obligation remains visible.

The invoice retains the exact qualification version, installation, binding fingerprint, completed service, support grant, physical hardware receipt, gross retainer, credit, and earned amount. Unique contract/week invoice IDs and posted-fact evidence prevent replay from issuing the retainer twice. Credits reduce the earned retainer; they are not another cash expense posted on top of a reduced bill.

Tenant token counts explain its completed work. They do not enter the public API's input/output token invoice, change public fractional billing carry, or become additional consented customer-feedback cases. The public product keeps its original deployment and billing contract. Existing private hosting, customer-hosted weight licensing, consumer subscriptions, and agent billing retain their own meters and rights rules.

## Migration and independent compatibility

A valid Wave 12 import changes only content/build headers from **0.13.0 to 0.14.0**, returns migration marker **13**, and keeps schema **5**. It does not advance time, accept a buyer, initialize empty assurance collections, alter resource balances, or rewrite existing evidence. The only new optional product collections are `assuranceContracts` and `assuranceInvoices`.

Before the first upgraded primary browser write, the exact Wave 12 source text is archived under **`frontier-lab.wave12.original`**. Existing original archives remain untouched. A failed archive prevents replacement; a failed primary write retains both the original primary and any successful archive. The recovery UI exposes **Export original Wave 12 save**. Older generations retain their existing independent migration and recovery paths.

The compatibility oracle was captured with an archived copy of the exact published Wave 12 engine before Wave 13 implementation. Its fixture is **Week 443**, checksum **`4f0d5c15`**, with **1,864,282 snapshot bytes** before the fixture file's trailing newline. A strict replacement has consumed one of its two development CUw and one of its two ML staff-weeks; the older checker is stale. Existing feedback, invoice proofs, and release state are already present.

The new engine reproduces **21 independently captured complete state hashes for Weeks 444–464**, normalizing only content/build headers for comparison. A second continuation saves and reloads before every step. These comparisons include completion and next-boundary audit publication, restoration of protection, paid release evidence, checker disable/reenable, public billing carry, retained feedback, cost-history expiry, and five additional legitimate rollouts to the product's eight-installation limit.

The **2,000,000-byte UTF-8 limit** remains unchanged for imports and saves. Every Wave 0–12 content header rejects both new collections even when empty, as well as signing, requalification, and cancellation commands. Checksums are checked before migration, future unsupported versions are rejected, and relabeling genuine Wave 12 runtime work as Wave 11 remains invalid. All thirteen supported prior generations migrate without automatic assurance adoption.
